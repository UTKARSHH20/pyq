  • Schedule Y: Requirements and guidelines for clinical trials, import, and manufacture of new drugs.

Must-Memorize Statutory Definitions

  • Spurious Drug (Sec 17B, D&C Act): Imported/manufactured under a name belonging to another drug, is an imitation/substitute, bears fictitious manufacturer name, or purports to be a product of a manufacturer of whom it is not truly a product.
  • Adulterated Drug (Sec 17A): Consists in whole or part of filthy, putrid, or decomposed substance; prepared/packed under insanitary conditions; container composed of poisonous/deleterious substance.
  • Misbranded Drug (Sec 17): So coloured, coated, powdered, or polished that damage is concealed; not labelled in prescribed manner; label bears false or misleading statement.
  • Magic Remedy (Sec 2(c), DMR Act): Includes a talisman, mantra, kavacha, and any other charm of any kind alleged to possess miraculous powers for diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment, or prevention of disease in human beings or animals.
  • Registered Pharmacist (Sec 2(i), Pharmacy Act): A person whose name is for the time being entered in the register of the State in which he is for the time being residing or carrying on his profession or business of pharmacy.
  • Loan Licence (Rule 69A/75A): A licence granted to a person who does not have his own manufacturing arrangements but intends to avail himself of the manufacturing facilities owned by another licensee.
  • Repacking Licence (Rule 69B): A licence granted for the process of breaking up any drug from a bulk container into small packages and labelling each package with a view to its sale and distribution (excluding Schedule C & C1).

Model 10-Mark Exam Answer

1. Introduction & Objectives of the Pharmacy Act, 1948

The Pharmacy Act, 1948 was enacted to regulate the profession and practice of pharmacy in India. Its primary objectives are:

  • To raise the status of the profession of pharmacy in India by establishing uniform educational standards.
  • To constitute the Pharmacy Council of India (PCI) at the central level to regulate pharmacy education (Education Regulations).
  • To constitute State Pharmacy Councils (and Joint State Pharmacy Councils) for the registration of pharmacists and regulation of professional conduct.
  • To restrict the compounding, preparing, mixing, or dispensing of medicines on prescriptions solely to Registered Pharmacists.

2. Constitution of Pharmacy Council of India (PCI / Central Council — Section 3)

The Central Government constitutes the PCI every 5 years, consisting of three categories of members:

  • A. Elected Members:
    • 6 members elected by the University Grants Commission (UGC) from among teachers of pharmacy, pharmaceutical chemistry, pharmacology, and pharmacognosy on the teaching staff of an Indian university or affiliated college (at least one teacher from each of the four subjects).
    • 1 member elected by the Medical Council of India (now National Medical Commission) from among its members.
    • 1 member elected by each State Pharmacy Council, who shall be a Registered Pharmacist.
  • B. Nominated Members:
    • 6 members nominated by the Central Government, of whom at least 4 must possess a degree or diploma in pharmacy or pharmaceutical chemistry or be registered pharmacists.
    • 1 representative each from the University Grants Commission (UGC) and the All India Council for Technical Education (AICTE).
    • 1 member nominated by each State Government/Union Territory administration, who shall be a Registered Pharmacist.
  • C. Ex-Officio Members:
    • The Director General of Health Services (DGHS).
    • The Drugs Controller General of India (DCGI).
    • The Director of the Central Drugs Laboratory (CDL), Kolkata.

3. Functions of the Pharmacy Council of India (PCI)

  • Framing Education Regulations (Section 10): Prescribing the minimum standard of education required for qualification as a pharmacist (nature and period of study, practical training, equipment, and examination standards).
  • Approval of Institutions & Examinations (Section 12): Inspecting pharmacy institutions through PCI Inspectors and granting or withdrawing approval of courses of study and examinations.
  • Approval of Foreign Qualifications (Section 14): Recognizing pharmacy qualifications granted outside India for registration purposes.
  • Maintenance of Central Register (Section 15A): Maintaining the Central Register of Pharmacists containing names of all persons entered in State Registers.

4. Constitution & Functions of State Pharmacy Council (Section 19) & Joint State Pharmacy Council (Section 20)

  • State Pharmacy Council Constitution:
    • Elected: 6 members elected from amongst themselves by Registered Pharmacists of the State; 1 member elected by the State Medical Council.
    • Nominated: 5 members nominated by the State Government (at least 3 possessing a degree/diploma in pharmacy or registered pharmacists).
    • Ex-Officio: Chief Administrative Medical Officer of the State; Officer-in-charge of Drugs Control Administration of the State; Government Analyst under D&C Act, 1940.
  • Joint State Pharmacy Council (Section 20): Two or more State Governments may enter into an agreement (for a specified period) to constitute a Joint State Pharmacy Council with elected (3–5 pharmacists per State; 1 per State Medical Council), nominated (2–4 per State Government), and ex-officio members from each participating State.
  • Key Functions: Preparation and maintenance of the State Register of Pharmacists, renewal of registration, removal of names for professional misconduct/conviction, and enforcement of the Pharmacy Act within the State.

Model 10-Mark Exam Answer

1. Objectives of the Medicinal and Toilet Preparations (Excise Duties) Act, 1955

  • To provide for the levy and collection of uniform duties of excise on medicinal and toilet preparations containing alcohol, opium, Indian hemp, or other narcotic drugs/narcotics throughout India.
  • To eliminate inter-state smuggling and disparity in excise duty rates across different Indian states.
  • To regulate the manufacture, storage, warehousing, and movement of alcohol-containing formulations to prevent misuse of rectified spirit for potable (beverage) purposes.

2. Manufacture in a Bonded Laboratory (Manufacture in Bond)

A Bonded Manufactory is a licensed premises approved by the Excise Commissioner where spirit is received and stored without prior payment of excise duty (under bond), and duty is paid only when the finished preparations are cleared from the bond.

  • Premises & Security Requirements: Must have separate rooms for (a) rectified spirit store, (b) manufacturing room, (c) finished goods store, and (d) Excise Officer's office. All windows must have iron bars (not less than 19 mm thick, max 102 mm apart) with wire netting; only one entrance to the bonded laboratory and one door to each compartment, secured by excise ticket locks.
  • Procurement of Rectified Spirit: Manufacturer applies to the Excise Officer in prescribed form; spirit is issued from a distillery/warehouse under bond without duty payment, accompanied by a transport permit.
  • Verification & Storage: On arrival, the Excise Officer verifies volume and proof strength and stores the spirit in the Spirit Store under excise lock.
  • Manufacturing Procedure: Manufacturer submits a requisition to the Excise Officer specifying quantity of spirit needed and formulation batch details. Spirit is issued to the manufacturing room, mixed immediately with other ingredients in the presence of the Excise Officer, and finished batches are transferred to the Finished Store.
  • Clearance & Payment of Duty: Samples are tested by the Chemical Examiner for alcohol strength. Duty is paid before removal from the Finished Store (or goods are exported under bond without duty).

3. Manufacture in a Non-Bonded Laboratory (Manufacture Outside Bond)

A Non-Bonded Manufactory is a licensed premises where rectified spirit is purchased only after paying full excise duty upfront.

  • Premises Requirements: Simpler layout than bonded labs; requires separate spirit store, laboratory, and finished store with barred windows and wire mesh, but is not locked by Excise Officers.
  • Procurement of Spirit: Manufacturer pays excise duty in advance into the Government Treasury, submits the treasury challan to the Excise Officer, and obtains a permit to purchase duty-paid spirit from an approved distillery.
  • Manufacturing & Record Keeping: Manufacturing is conducted during prescribed hours without continuous physical supervision of an Excise Officer, though the officer may inspect periodically and draw samples. Strict stock registers (Spirit Account, Manufacturing Register, Sales Register) must be maintained.

4. Key Comparison: Bonded vs. Non-Bonded Laboratory

Feature Bonded Laboratory Non-Bonded Laboratory
Excise Duty Payment Paid after manufacture, at the time of clearance of finished goods. Paid in advance before purchasing rectified spirit.
Excise Supervision Continuous supervision by a posted Excise Officer; doors secured with excise locks. Periodic inspection by Excise Officer; no excise locks on doors.
Capital Requirement Lower working capital blocked in duty; higher infrastructure & security bond cost. Higher working capital needed upfront for duty-paid spirit.

1. Definition of Magic Remedy (Section 2(c))

A "Magic Remedy" includes a talisman, mantra, kavacha, and any other charm of any kind which is alleged to possess miraculous powers for or in the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment, or prevention of any disease in human beings or animals, or for affecting or influencing in any way the structure or any organic function of the body of human beings or animals.

2. Objectives of the Act

  • To control the advertisement of drugs in certain cases and protect the public from self-medication with harmful or ineffective drugs.
  • To prohibit advertisements of remedies alleged to possess magic qualities.
  • To prevent unethical exploitation of gullible patients suffering from incurable or chronic ailments.

3. Classes of Prohibited Advertisements (Sections 3, 4 & 5)

  • Section 3 (Prohibition of advertisement of certain drugs for treatment of certain diseases): Prohibits advertisements referring to any drug in terms calculated to lead to its use for:
    1. Procurement of miscarriage in women or prevention of conception in women.
    2. Maintenance or improvement of the capacity of human beings for sexual pleasure.
    3. Correction of menstrual disorders in women.
    4. Diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment, or prevention of any disease/disorder specified in the Schedule (54 diseases including AIDS, cancer, diabetes, blindness, cataract, leprosy, insanity, epilepsy, tuberculosis, venereal diseases, asthma, high/low blood pressure).
  • Section 4 (Misleading Advertisements): Prohibits advertisements that directly or indirectly give a false impression regarding the true character of a drug, make false claims for the drug, or are otherwise false or misleading in any material particular.
  • Section 5 (Magic Remedies): Prohibits any person carrying on or purporting to carry on the profession of administering magic remedies from publishing any advertisement referring to any magic remedy for the diseases listed in Section 3.

4. Classes of Exempted Advertisements (Section 14 & Rules)

  • Any signboard or notice displayed by a Registered Medical Practitioner on his premises indicating that treatment for any disease/disorder specified in Section 3 is undertaken there.
  • Any treatise or book dealing with any matter relating to Section 3 diseases from a bona fide scientific or social standpoint.
  • Any advertisement relating to a drug sent confidentially in the prescribed manner to a Registered Medical Practitioner (bearing the words "For the use only of registered medical practitioners or a hospital or a laboratory").
  • Any advertisement relating to a drug printed or published by the Government, or by any person with the previous sanction of the Government.
  • Labels or leaflets enclosed inside the retail package of a drug containing information required under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940.

Model 10-Mark Exam Answer

1. Introduction & Objectives of the PCA Act, 1960

The Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (PCA) Act, 1960 was enacted to prevent the infliction of unnecessary pain or suffering on animals and to amend laws relating to prevention of cruelty to animals. Chapter IV (Sections 14–20) specifically regulates experimentation on animals for the advancement of physiological knowledge and discovery of new drugs.

2. CPCSEA (Committee for the Purpose of Control and Supervision of Experiments on Animals)

Constituted by the Central Government under Section 15 of the PCA Act, 1960 (now officially renamed CCSEA), CPCSEA is the apex statutory body responsible for ensuring that animals are not subjected to unnecessary pain or suffering before, during, or after the performance of experiments on them.

  • Registration: Every establishment breeding animals for experiments or conducting animal experiments must register with CPCSEA.
  • Procurement: Animals must be procured only from CPCSEA-registered breeders; capture from the wild or purchase of stray animals is prohibited.

3. CPCSEA Guidelines for Animal Breeding & Experimentation

  • Principle of 4Rs: Replacement (use of alternatives where possible), Reduction (minimum number of animals for statistically valid results), Refinement (minimizing pain/distress), and Rehabilitation (post-experiment care/rehabilitation of larger animals).
  • Qualification & Supervision: Experiments must be performed by or under the direct supervision of qualified scientists/veterinarians.
  • Use of Anaesthesia & Analgesia: Operations likely to cause pain must be performed under appropriate anaesthesia; if pain persists after anaesthesia wears off, the animal must be painlessly euthanized before regaining consciousness.
  • Housing & Husbandry: Animals must be housed in clean, well-ventilated, temperature- and humidity-controlled animal houses with adequate species-specific cage space, clean drinking water, balanced diet, and veterinary care.
  • Record Keeping: Detailed records of animal procurement, breeding, experimental protocols, morbidity/mortality, and disposal must be maintained for CPCSEA inspection.

4. Functions of Institutional Animal Ethics Committee (IAEC)

Every registered establishment must constitute an IAEC (comprising biological scientists, a veterinarian, a non-scientific socially aware member, and a CPCSEA nominee):

  • Review and approve/reject research protocols involving small laboratory animals (rodents, rabbits, guinea pigs) prior to commencement. (Protocols involving large animals like dogs, primates, equines require direct CPCSEA sub-committee approval after IAEC recommendation).
  • Ensure adherence to CPCSEA guidelines, anaesthesia protocols, and humane endpoints.
  • Conduct periodic inspections of the Institutional Animal House facility and verify animal health and stock registers.
  • Suspend or revoke approval of any ongoing experiment if non-compliance or unnecessary animal suffering is observed.

1. Appointment & Qualifications of Drug Inspector (Section 21 & Rule 49)

Appointed by the Central or State Government by notification in the Official Gazette. A person must hold a degree in Pharmacy or Pharmaceutical Sciences or Medicine with specialization in Clinical Pharmacology or Microbiology from a recognized university. (For inspecting Schedule C & C1 manufacturing, at least 18 months' experience in manufacture/testing of Schedule C substances or 3 years' inspection experience is required).

2. Powers of Drug Inspector (Section 22)

  • Inspect any premises wherein any drug or cosmetic is being manufactured, sold, stocked, exhibited, or distributed.
  • Take samples of any drug or cosmetic being manufactured, sold, or in transit.
  • Search any person, vehicle, vessel, or place where he has reason to believe an offence under the Act is being committed.
  • Seize stock of drugs/cosmetics, registers, records, or documents (in Form 16) if contravening the Act, and issue an order in writing (Form 15) directing the person not to dispose of any stock for a period not exceeding 20 days.
  • Examine any record, register, or document and institute prosecutions for breaches of the Act.

3. Duties of Drug Inspector (Rules 51 & 52)

  • Inspection of Sale Premises (Rule 51): Inspect at least twice a year all establishments licensed for sale of drugs within his assigned area to verify compliance with licence conditions.
  • Inspection of Manufacturing Premises (Rule 52): Inspect at least twice a year all premises licensed for manufacture of drugs (especially Schedule C, C1, and M compliance) and submit detailed inspection reports to the Controlling Authority.
  • Send samples of suspected drugs to the Government Analyst and investigate written complaints.
  • Maintain a record of all inspections made and actions taken, and make inquiries regarding unlawful sales or spurious drugs.

4. Procedure of Inspection & Sampling (Section 23)

  1. Entry & Verification: Present identity card; verify display of valid retail/wholesale/manufacturing licences (Forms 20, 21, 20B, 21B, 25, 28) and presence of a Registered Pharmacist (in retail) or approved Technical Staff (in manufacturing).
  2. Premises & Record Audit: Check storage conditions (cold chain/refrigerator logs for Schedule C/C1), expiry date registers, Schedule H/H1/X registers, purchase invoices, and batch manufacturing/testing records (BMR/BTR) in factories.
  3. Sampling Procedure (Section 23):
    • Pay the fair price of the sample taken and obtain a written acknowledgement.
    • Intimate the purpose in writing in Form 17 to the person from whom the sample is taken.
    • Divide the sample into 4 portions (or 3 portions if taken from a manufacturing premises) in the presence of the person, effectively seal and mark each portion, and allow the person to add his own seal/mark.
    • Distribution of Portions: (i) One portion handed over to the person from whom it was taken; (ii) One portion sent to the Government Analyst (with Memorandum in Form 18); (iii) One portion retained for production in Court if required; (iv) Fourth portion (for retail/sale samples) sent to the warrantor/manufacturer whose name appears on the label.

Model 10-Mark Exam Answer

1. Introduction to Schedule M

Schedule M of the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945 prescribes the Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) and requirements of factory premises, plant, and equipment for the manufacture of pharmaceutical products in India. It ensures that products are consistently produced and controlled to quality standards appropriate to their intended use.

2. Structure of Schedule M

  • Part I: Good Manufacturing Practices for Premises and Materials (General requirements + Specific requirements for Sterile Products, Oral Solid Dosage Forms, Oral Liquids, Topical Products, Metered-Dose Inhalers, and APIs).
  • Part II: Requirements of Plant and Equipment for various dosage forms.

3. Key Components of GMP under Schedule M

  • Location & Surroundings: Factory must be situated away from open sewage, drains, public lavatories, or factories emitting obnoxious fumes, smoke, or excessive dust.
  • Buildings & Premises: Designed to prevent cross-contamination, mix-ups, and entry of insects/rodents. Walls, floors, and ceilings must be smooth, impervious, washable, and free from cracks. Separate dedicated areas for receiving, quarantine, sampling, storage, manufacturing, packaging, and Quality Control.
  • Water System & HVAC: Validated purified water and Water for Injection (WFI) systems; dedicated Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning (HVAC) with HEPA filters and pressure differentials for sterile/penicillin/cephalosporin areas.
  • Warehousing & Raw Material Control: Quarantine area for untested raw materials; approved storage with temperature/humidity monitoring; FIFO (First-In, First-Out) and FEFO (First-Expiry, First-Out) inventory rotation.
  • Sanitation & Hygiene: Medical examination of personnel, clean protective garments, hand-washing facilities, and prohibition of eating, drinking, or smoking in production areas.
  • Quality Control (QC) & Quality Assurance (QA): Independent QC laboratory equipped for chemical, microbiological, and instrumental testing; batch release only after QA approval; retention of reference samples.
  • Documentation & Validation: Master Formula Records (MFR), Batch Manufacturing Records (BMR), Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), and process/equipment validation logs preserved for at least 1 year after batch expiry (or 3 years, whichever is longer).
  • Self-Inspection & Product Recall: Periodic internal GMP audits and a documented system for rapid recall of defective batches from the market.

1. Introduction

The Code of Pharmaceutical Ethics is formulated by the Pharmacy Council of India (PCI) to guide pharmacists in their professional duties toward the public, medical profession, and fellow pharmacists, ensuring high moral and professional standards beyond minimum statutory laws.

2. Four Pillars of the Code of Pharmaceutical Ethics

  • A. Pharmacist in Relation to His Job:
    • Scope of Pharmaceutical Service: Provide comprehensive, uninterrupted, and prompt pharmaceutical care without undue delay.
    • Conduct of the Pharmacy: Maintain the pharmacy cleanly and systematically to prevent accidental contamination or dispensing errors.

9. Key Legal Procedures & DPCO Price Calculation Formulas

A. DPCO Formulation Price Calculations (Relevant to Nov-22 Part C Q3(g))

Additional Legal Verification Note: In Indian B.Pharm exams, questions asking "How are the prices of formulations to the wholesale and retailer calculated?" are answered using either the current DPCO 2013 Market-Based Pricing trade-margin structure or the classical DPCO 1995 Cost-Based (MAPE) formula taught in standard textbooks (Kuchekar / Mithal). Presenting both ensures full marks:

1. Current DPCO 2013 Framework (NPPA Market-Based Ceiling Price)

  • Step 1 — Average Price to Retailer (Ps): Simple average of Price to Retailer (PTR) of all brands/generics of the scheduled formulation having a market share of 1% or more of total market turnover.
  • Step 2 — Ceiling Price (Pc): Add 16% Retailer Margin to Ps:
    Pc = Ps × (1 + 16/100) = 1.16 × Ps
  • Step 3 — Maximum Retail Price (MRP): MRP = Pc + Applicable Local Taxes (GST).
  • Step 4 — Price to Retailer (PTR) & Price to Wholesaler (PTW):
    PTR = Pc / 1.16 (or MRP excluding GST − 16% margin)
    PTW = PTR × (1 − 0.08) (allowing standard 8% Wholesaler Margin on PTR).

2. Classical DPCO Formula (Cost-Based MAPE Formula — Taught in Standard Textbooks)

RP = (MC + CC + PM + PC) × (1 + MAPE / 100) + ED

  • RP: Retail Price | MC: Material Cost | CC: Conversion Cost | PM: Cost of Packing Material | PC: Packing Charges | MAPE: Maximum Allowable Post-Manufacturing Expenses (100% for indigenously manufactured scheduled formulations) | ED: Excise Duty / GST.
  • Worked Example: Suppose (MC + CC + PM + PC) = ₹40, MAPE = 100%, and Tax/ED = ₹5:
    Retail Price (RP) = 40 × (1 + 100/100) + 5 = 40 × 2 + 5 = ₹85.00.
    Allowing 16% retailer margin on pre-tax price (₹80): Price to Retailer (PTR) = 80 − (16% of 80) = ₹67.20.
    Allowing 8% wholesaler margin: Price to Wholesaler (PTW) = 67.20 − (8% of 67.20) = ₹61.82.

B. Chronological Flowchart: Pharmacist Registration (Pharmacy Act, 1948 — Sec 32–34)

  1. Application: Candidate possessing approved D.Pharm/B.Pharm degree + 500 hrs practical training applies in prescribed form to the Registrar of the State Pharmacy Council with fee, age proof (≥18 yrs), residence/business proof, and original certificates.
  2. Scrutiny by Registrar: Registrar verifies authenticity of qualifications from PCI-approved institution and checks character/conviction records.
  3. Entry in State Register: If satisfied, the Registrar enters the applicant's name, residential address, date of admission, and qualifications in the State Register of Pharmacists.
  4. Issue of Registration Certificate: Registrar issues a formal Certificate of Registration (valid for display at the pharmacy). (If rejected, applicant may appeal to the State Pharmacy Council within 3 months).
  5. Annual Renewal & Central Register: Renewed annually upon payment of retention fee; copies of the State Register are forwarded to PCI for entry into the Central Register of Pharmacists.
